# Concept Catalog and Rotation

## Read the Tracker First

Before picking a concept, read `progress-ledger.md`. Look at the last 4-6 rows for: which concepts, which categories, and what difficulty were used, and what the previous row's "Recommended next concept" said. That recommendation is a strong default, not a hard rule — override it if the user names a concept explicitly.

## Rotation Rules

Unless the user explicitly requests a concept:

1. Do not repeat the same concept in two consecutive sessions.
2. Rotate across categories — don't pick from the same category two sessions running unless the ledger shows every other category was covered more recently.
3. Prefer a concept not covered in the last 3-4 sessions over one covered recently.
4. Default to intermediate difficulty. Step down to beginner if the ledger's performance observations show the learner struggling broadly (multiple low self-scores across dimensions in recent sessions); step up to advanced if recent sessions show consistently strong self-scores (4+ average) across 2-3 sessions running.
5. If the user names an industry, company, concept, or difficulty explicitly, honor it and log it as such — the rotation logic is a default, not a constraint on the user.
6. When triggered by a Cowork automation with no user present, always apply rules 1-4 with no exceptions (there's no one to ask).

## Concept Catalog by Category

Pick one concept per session. Keep it narrow enough to teach and practice in one sitting — never combine two catalog entries into one shallow session.

**Product Sense**
User segmentation · Identifying user needs · Selecting a target segment · Problem prioritization · Solution ideation · Solution prioritization · MVP definition · Product vision · Product differentiation

**Product Strategy**
Market selection · Competitive analysis · Build, buy, or partner · Platform strategy · Ecosystem strategy · Monetization · Pricing · Market entry · Growth strategy · Network effects

**Product Execution**
North Star metrics · Input and output metrics · Guardrail metrics · Funnel analysis · Root-cause analysis · Diagnosing metric changes · Experiment design · A/B testing · Trade-off decisions · Launch planning

**Prioritization**
RICE · ICE · Impact vs effort · Opportunity scoring · Kano model · Cost of delay · Strategic alignment · Risk-adjusted prioritization

**Product Analytics**
Acquisition/activation/retention/revenue/referral (AARRR) · Cohort analysis · Retention curves · Conversion funnels · Leading vs lagging indicators · Marketplace metrics · Subscription metrics · Engagement metrics · Quality and reliability metrics

**AI Product Management**
AI use-case selection · Human-in-the-loop design · Model evaluation metrics · AI product failure modes · Trust and safety · AI agent design · Automation vs augmentation · Model quality vs product quality · AI cost/latency/accuracy trade-offs

## Company Selection for the Observe Module

Prefer a company whose product, customers, business model, and the specific challenge are understandable from credible public sources: Airbnb, Amazon, Atlassian, Canva, DoorDash, Duolingo, Figma, Google, HubSpot, LinkedIn, Microsoft, Netflix, Notion, OpenAI, Pinterest, Shopify, Slack, Spotify, Stripe, Uber, Zoom, or another company when it's a better fit for the concept and has strong public documentation (investor materials, engineering/product blog, shareholder letters, reputable reporting). Don't reuse the same company two sessions in a row for the Observe module.
