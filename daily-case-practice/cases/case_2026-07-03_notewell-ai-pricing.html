NoteWell AI — pricing strategy under margin squeeze

Pricing / Growth Strategy Healthcare + AI Difficulty: Medium-Hard ~35 min, full case

Prompt

NoteWell AI sells an ambient AI scribe — a tool that listens to doctor-patient visits and auto-drafts the clinical note — to mid-sized hospital systems. NoteWell charges a flat $150 per provider per month subscription.

Two forces are squeezing NoteWell from both sides. Low-cost standalone apps now start around $40/provider/month and are peeling off small practices. At the same time, enterprise players like Abridge, Augmedix, and Microsoft's DAX Copilot (list price $500+/provider/month) are winning the largest health systems by bundling in EHR integration, coding/revenue-cycle support, and compliance guarantees that NoteWell can't yet match.

NoteWell's CEO has asked your team: should we change our pricing model to defend and grow our position in the mid-market hospital segment?

  • No specific revenue or margin target has been set — open-ended strategic question
  • NoteWell has three rough customer tiers: small outpatient practices, mid-market hospital systems, and large enterprise health systems
  • Gross margin on the current subscription is ~78% (cloud inference + support costs)

Step 2 — Build your framework

Take 60-90 seconds. Write the buckets you'd use to structure this pricing decision before revealing a model answer.


A reasonable structure

1. Customer segmentation & willingness to pay — how do small practices, mid-market systems, and large enterprise systems differ in what they'll pay and why?

2. Value delivered vs. price — is $150/provider priced against time-saved, or against downstream revenue capture (coding accuracy, visit volume)?

3. Competitive positioning — where does NoteWell sit between the $40 budget tier and the $500+ enterprise tier, and is the middle a viable place to live?

4. Pricing model options — per-seat vs. per-visit vs. outcome/revenue-share vs. tiered bundling.

5. Implementation & risk — margin impact, sales complexity, customer reaction, competitive response.

Step 3 — Brainstorm

Q: The mid-market hospital systems in the middle are the ones churning. What might be driving them toward the budget tier or away from NoteWell entirely? Generate at least 4-5 ideas.


Price-driven: budget apps are 3-4x cheaper and "good enough" for lower-acuity specialties; procurement pressure from hospital CFOs post-pandemic margin squeeze.

Value-driven: mid-market systems don't have the EHR/coding integration that makes the enterprise tier's revenue-capture case compelling, so they don't see $150 fully justified either.

Product-driven: budget tools may now match core transcription quality; NoteWell's differentiation (compliance, integration) may matter more to large systems than mid-market ones.

Switching-cost driven: low integration depth today means low switching costs — nothing locks a mid-market customer in once price becomes the deciding factor.

Trust/adoption driven: physician skepticism after mixed results in published trials (modest, inconsistent time savings) may make CFOs question whether the premium price is worth it.

Step 5 — Quant exercise

Q: NoteWell is considering replacing the flat $150/provider/month fee with a hybrid model: $60/provider/month base fee + $2 per completed visit note. For a typical mid-market hospital system, estimate whether this hybrid model raises or lowers NoteWell's revenue per system, and by how much. Ask the interviewer for any data you need.

Ask: providers per system Ask: visits per provider Ask: working days/month
Average mid-market system has 120 providers on NoteWell.
Each provider sees roughly 18 patient visits per working day, and NoteWell generates a note for about 85% of those visits (some visits are too short or off-platform).
Assume 20 working days per month per provider.

The hybrid model roughly 4.5x's monthly revenue per system ($80,640 vs $18,000) under these volume assumptions — because visit volume (2,160 visits/month per provider) is high relative to the $150 flat fee, so a $2/note charge scales revenue much faster than a seat fee.

Sanity check / advanced comment: this is almost certainly too large a price increase to survive a renewal conversation — a 4.5x price hike would trigger heavy pushback or defection, especially from budget-sensitive mid-market CFOs already flagged as churn risks. The math shows the hybrid model directionally favors NoteWell if usage is high, but the actual per-note rate would need to be reverse-engineered from a target realistic increase (e.g. 10-20%) rather than accepted at face value — a good candidate flags this instead of just reporting the raw number.

Step 6 — Recommendation

Pull it together: what should NoteWell's CEO do, and what's the biggest risk?


NoteWell should not adopt the hybrid model as calculated — the math reveals it's mispriced, not that usage-based pricing is wrong. Better path: introduce a tiered structure — keep a lower base seat fee for low-usage/small practices, add a modest per-note component calibrated to roughly a 10-15% blended increase, and reserve the premium enterprise bundle (coding/EHR integration) for large systems willing to pay closer to the $300-500 range.

Biggest risk: repricing during a period of elevated churn and mixed clinical-outcome data could accelerate defection rather than stem it — any change should be piloted with a subset of stable mid-market accounts (the highest-revenue-at-risk segment from the chart) and paired with clearer ROI proof points before a broad rollout.

Real-world grounding: enterprise ambient scribe pricing is genuinely contract-priced and can exceed $500/provider/month, while budget tools start near $40 — the mid-market squeeze in this case mirrors the real pricing spread reported in the ambient AI scribe market in 2026.
